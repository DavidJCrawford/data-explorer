# Inherited

F1 Analysis's editorial voice applies: third person, declarative, no
marketing superlatives, no forced contrast ("Not X. Y."), no aphoristic
triplets, sparing em-dashes, curly quotes, flush left. The Longest Journey's
rule applies too: passages are for readers, not about the site.

# Exceptions, argued

- **Rights are in the second person.** "The data your building makes is yours
  to take." A right is addressed to someone; third person would turn it into
  a description of a law. This is limited to the seven rights' statements and
  the stop cards' "owed here" lines.
- **The prototype's locked phrases** are kept where they are claims, and
  dropped where they are rhythm. Kept: "Custody is a path, not a promise"
  (the trace's subtitle candidate) and "Both architectures now owe the
  customer open doors. Only one can answer for everything behind them."
  Each is used once.

# Audience

Facilities managers, and the architects and engineers who specify building
systems. Not lawyers, not security specialists. Words to avoid or explain on
first use: head-end, controller (say what it does), related service, data
holder, sub-processor. The Act's own terms are used where precision matters
and glossed in plain words beside them.

# Numbers and dates

Dates in prose as "12 January 2027"; ISO 8601 in data. Article references as
"Article 29" in prose and "Art. 29" only in compact labels.
