# Decision

David, 2026-10-09: reimagine the experience; do not reuse the node-graph
concept. Content carries over; the form does not.

# Why

The node graph showed that things connect. It could not show the order data
travels in, where it physically is, or how many organisations hold it, which
is the article's argument. A graph of fifty articles also put the statute
first, where the facilities manager's building should be.

# What replaces it

- **The building** becomes an [architectural section](/design/the-section-drawing.md)
  that a door event travels through: motion with a direction and an order.
- **The law** becomes [the rulebook](../../SPEC.md): instruments laid as bands
  along the same hops, with a time axis, not a graph.

# What survives

All of the content: the fifty articles in two registers, the seven rights,
the components and their attachments, the wider rulebook. Also the drawer's
two-register pattern, ELI citations, agent parity, and the no-JS reference.
