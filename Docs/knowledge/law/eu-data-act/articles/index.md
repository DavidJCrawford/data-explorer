# The articles of the EU Data Act

Fifty articles in eleven chapters. Each concept carries two registers: a precise reading (a faithful summary, not the statute) and a plain reading (paraphrase). Readings are the author's, not legal advice.


# Chapter I — General Provisions

* [Article 1 — Subject matter and scope](article-01.md) - The perimeter: if you put connected hardware or its companion services on the EU market, this law reaches you, wherever you are based.
* [Article 2 — Definitions](article-02.md) - The load-bearing words. Most arguments about this Act are arguments about these definitions — start here.

# Chapter II — Business to Consumer and Business to Business Data Sharing

* [Article 3 — Obligation to make product data and related service data accessible to the user](article-03.md) - Data by design becomes a product requirement, like safety. The building's edge devices must be born legible to their owner.
* [Article 4 — The rights and obligations of users and data holders with regard to access, use and making available product data and related service data](article-04.md) - Whatever the vendor can see of your system's data, you can demand — free, machine-readable, live. The custody question stops being 'may I?' and becomes 'how well?'.
* [Article 5 — Right of the user to share data with third parties](article-05.md) - Your data goes wherever you point — including to the vendor's competitor. Lock-in by possession is repealed; being worth choosing is what remains.
* [Article 6 — Obligations of third parties receiving data at the request of the user](article-06.md) - The receiving party is a steward too: purpose-bound, no onward leakage, no cloning the product it came from.
* [Article 7 — Scope of business-to-consumer and business-to-business data sharing obligations](article-07.md) - The duties scale with the size of the party holding the data — and cannot be waived in the fine print.

# Chapter III — Obligations for Data Holders Obliged to Make Data Available Pursuant to Union Law

* [Article 8 — Conditions under which data holders make data available to data recipients](article-08.md) - FRAND comes to device data: when sharing is owed, the terms cannot be a second wall around the moat.
* [Article 9 — Compensation for making data available](article-09.md) - Data access has a price tag, but a regulated one: cost-based for the small, transparent for everyone.
* [Article 10 — Dispute settlement](article-10.md) - A referee short of a courtroom, for when the steward and the recipient can't agree the terms.
* [Article 11 — Technical protection measures on the unauthorised use or disclosure of data](article-11.md) - Protection is legitimate; protection-as-obstruction is not. The lock guards the agreement, never the exit.
* [Article 12 — Scope of obligations for data holders obliged pursuant to Union law to make data available](article-12.md) - The FRAND machinery attaches to new sharing obligations going forward, not retroactively.

# Chapter IV — Unfair Contractual Terms Related to Data Access and Use Between Enterprises

* [Article 13 — Unfair contractual terms unilaterally imposed on another enterprise](article-13.md) - Take-it-or-leave-it custody clauses lose their teeth. The stewardship posture — clear terms both sides would sign in daylight — becomes the only stable strategy.

# Chapter V — Making Data Available to Public Sector Bodies, the Commission, the ECB and Union Bodies on the Basis of an Exceptional Need

* [Article 14 — Obligation to make data available on the basis of an exceptional need](article-14.md) - The state can ask — but only through the narrow gate of exceptional need, defined next door.
* [Article 15 — Exceptional need to use data](article-15.md) - Emergencies, mostly. This is not a general public-sector data tap.
* [Article 16 — Relationship with other obligations to make data available to public sector bodies](article-16.md) - Housekeeping: this chapter adds a channel; it doesn't rewrite the others.
* [Article 17 — Requests for data to be made available](article-17.md) - The state's ask must arrive in writing, scoped and published — a well-formed request, or no request.
* [Article 18 — Compliance with requests for data](article-18.md) - Comply, or push back on listed grounds — quickly, either way.
* [Article 19 — Obligations of public sector bodies, the Commission, the ECB and Union bodies](article-19.md) - The state is held to stewardship rules too: purpose, protection, deletion.
* [Article 20 — Compensation in cases of an exceptional need](article-20.md) - Emergencies are pro bono; everything else is cost-plus.
* [Article 21 — Sharing of data obtained in the context of an exceptional need with research organisations or statistical bodies](article-21.md) - One hop further is allowed — to researchers, same leash.
* [Article 22 — Mutual assistance and cross-border cooperation](article-22.md) - Cross-border asks go through channels, not around them.

# Chapter VI — Switching Between Data Processing Services

* [Article 23 — Removing obstacles to effective switching](article-23.md) - The exit exists by law. Everything in this chapter is the removal of furniture piled against that door.
* [Article 24 — Scope of the technical obligations](article-24.md) - Who owes how much of the switching machinery, by service type.
* [Article 25 — Contractual terms concerning switching](article-25.md) - The exit is written into the contract with dates on it — retrieval, assistance, then provable erasure.
* [Article 26 — Information obligation of providers of data processing services](article-26.md) - The map to the exit must be printed and kept current.
* [Article 27 — Obligation of good faith](article-27.md) - No malicious compliance at the door.
* [Article 28 — Contractual transparency obligations on international access and transfer](article-28.md) - Where your data physically lives, and what shields it from foreign subpoenas — published, not whispered.
* [Article 29 — Gradual withdrawal of switching charges](article-29.md) - Egress-priced exits are a business model with an expiry date printed on it: 12 January 2027.
* [Article 30 — Technical aspects of switching](article-30.md) - Not just permission to leave — the technical means to arrive somewhere else, intact.
* [Article 31 — Specific regime for certain data processing services](article-31.md) - Bespoke systems get a carve-out — declared up front, not discovered at exit.

# Chapter VII — Unlawful International Governmental Access and Transfer of Non-Personal Data

* [Article 32 — International governmental access and transfer](article-32.md) - The sovereignty backstop: your data cannot be quietly reached across borders through your provider.

# Chapter VIII — Interoperability

* [Article 33 — Essential requirements regarding interoperability of data, of data sharing mechanisms and services, as well as of common European data spaces](article-33.md) - Interoperability as a duty of description: data legible enough to travel.
* [Article 34 — Interoperability for the purposes of in-parallel use of data processing services](article-34.md) - Not just leaving — running two at once must be possible too.
* [Article 35 — Interoperability of data processing services](article-35.md) - Where the market won't standardise the plumbing, Brussels can.
* [Article 36 — Essential requirements regarding smart contracts for executing data sharing agreements](article-36.md) - If code executes the agreement, the code carries duties: a kill switch, an audit trail, access control.

# Chapter IX — Implementation and Enforcement

* [Article 37 — Competent authorities and data coordinators](article-37.md) - Who enforces all this in each country — and the one desk (the coordinator) that fronts it.
* [Article 38 — Right to lodge a complaint](article-38.md) - The user's escalation path when the steward isn't one.
* [Article 39 — Right to an effective judicial remedy](article-39.md) - Courts remain above the machinery.
* [Article 40 — Penalties](article-40.md) - The teeth: GDPR-calibre fines stand behind the access and sharing duties.
* [Article 41 — Model contractual terms and standard contractual clauses](article-41.md) - Official templates, so fair terms have a starting point.
* [Article 42 — Role of the EDIB](article-42.md) - The coordination layer above the national desks.

# Chapter X — Sui Generis Right Under Directive 96/9/EC

* [Article 43 — Databases containing certain data](article-43.md) - A vendor cannot rebuild the possession moat by declaring the event log a 'protected database'. That door is closed by name.

# Chapter XI — Final Provisions

* [Article 44 — Other Union legal acts governing rights and obligations on data access and use](article-44.md) - The Act joins the lattice of EU data law; it doesn't replace it.
* [Article 45 — Exercise of the delegation](article-45.md) - Plumbing for future refinements.
* [Article 46 — Committee procedure](article-46.md) - More plumbing.
* [Article 47 — Amendment to Regulation (EU) 2017/2394](article-47.md) - Consumer authorities get this Act in their toolkit.
* [Article 48 — Amendment to Directive (EU) 2020/1828](article-48.md) - Class-style actions can enforce it.
* [Article 49 — Evaluation and review](article-49.md) - The law audits itself on a schedule.
* [Article 50 — Entry into force and application](article-50.md) - The clock: live since September 2025, design duties from September 2026, charge-free switching from January 2027 (Art. 29), and the fine print of old contracts caught from September 2027.
