# The claim

"Openness — your data, in your hands — stopped being a differentiator the day it
became a legal floor. Stewardship didn't."[^article]

Since 12 September 2025 the EU Data Act has given the user of a connected
product the right to its data ([Article 4](/law/eu-data-act/articles/article-04.md)),
the right to send it to anyone ([Article 5](/law/eu-data-act/articles/article-05.md)),
and the right to leave a cloud service on contractual terms that are written down ([Articles 23–31](/law/eu-data-act/chapters.md)), with switching charges
ending on 12 January 2027 ([Article 29](/law/eu-data-act/articles/article-29.md)).[^eli]
Products placed on the market since 12 September 2026 must be designed so the
data is accessible ([Article 3](/law/eu-data-act/articles/article-03.md)).

# What remains

If every vendor must open the door, no vendor wins by opening it. What still
separates them is what happens behind the door: how many hands the data passes
through, whether one party can account for all of it, and what is kept out of
the flow entirely.

# Not the last of its kind

"It won't be the last regulation of its kind."[^article] Other jurisdictions
have consumer data rights in specific sectors (see [jurisdictions](/law/jurisdictions/index.md)),
and none yet has a general right to connected-device data like the EU's. The
rulebook view shows that unevenly, on purpose.
